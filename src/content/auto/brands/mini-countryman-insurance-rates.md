## **What factors affect Mini Countryman**insurance**?**

Even though it may seem that insuring a smaller vehicle like the [Mini Countryman](<https://www.miniusa.com/model/countryman.html>) would be inexpensive, insurance companies take into account the fact that smaller automobiles tend to bear the brunt of the damage when involved in an accident. As a result, you may not receive the ultra-low rates you were hoping for.

Cars that cost more to purchase often cost more to insure. Mini vehicles may cost more to insure as a mid-tier brand than a Toyota or Honda, and the Countryman isn't on the low end of Mini models. The Mini Countryman, on the other hand, has a plethora of basic safety features that may help you save money on insurance.

The hybrid Countryman may be somewhat more expensive to buy than its non-hybrid cousin, but it is slightly less expensive to insure. Because many insurance companies provide a discount for hybrid vehicles, you may wind up with a lower premium if you go green.

## **Reliability and safety ratings for the Mini Countryman**

Typically, the Mini Countryman has an excellent safety rating from the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety, but its headlights get a mediocre grade due to restricted brightness choices.

The Countryman, like previous Minis, earned a 3 out of 5-star dependability rating from [JD Power](<https://jdpower.com>), but official safety ratings from JD Power are not yet available.

Smaller automobiles, such as the Countryman, tend to do well in crash testing, despite sustaining more external damage than bigger vehicles.

## **About the Mini Countryman**

The Mini Countryman is a hatchback subcompact crossover SUV with the form of a sedan and the practicality of an SUV. Launched in 2010, the Countryman was Small's first vehicle of this kind, and it garnered conflicting reviews regarding whether it was "mini" enough to suit the Mini name.

Mini vehicles used to use motors co-developed by [Chrysler](<https://chrysler.com>) and PSA Peugeot Citroen. However, because Mini has been owned by BMW since 2000, its motors are now manufactured by BMW, which may explain its comparatively high maintenance expenses.

If you're deciding between the Mini Cooper Countryman and its hybrid SE cousin, the hybrid choice offers slightly cheaper insurance rates and requires less regular maintenance.
