## **About the Nissan Pathfinder**

The [Nissan Pathfinder](<https://www.nissanusa.com/vehicles/crossovers-suvs/pathfinder.html>) has been on the market since 1985 and is now in its fourth generation of production. The midsize SUV is distinguished by its strong V6 engine and generous luggage space. The 2017 Nissan Pathfinder received a model year upgrade that included new headlights, new taillights, and a direct-injected V6 engine as standard equipment.

When determining insurance rates for a Nissan Pathfinder, insurance companies use factors like safety ratings, theft rates, replacement prices, and general damage susceptibility to decide the cost of coverage. Nissan Pathfinder insurance costs might fluctuate depending on your driving record and geographic area. In the event that you are considering purchasing a Nissan Pathfinder, obtain insurance quotes from multiple different providers to ensure that you are paying the lowest rate feasible. Check with your insurance provider to see if you qualify for any savings.

## **How safe is the Nissan Pathfinder?**

A 5-Star overall safety rating was awarded to the 2017 Nissan Pathfinder by the [National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA)](<https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle/2019/NISSAN/PATHFINDER/SUV/AWD>). The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration awarded the side impact test five stars, the best possible grade. In addition, the frontal crash test and the rollover crash test received four stars from the government agency. The Nissan Pathfinder has been designated as a Top Safety Pick vehicle by the [Insurance Institute for Highway Safety (IIHS)](<https://www.iihs.org/ratings/vehicle/nissan/pathfinder-4-door-suv/2020>). The IIHS gave the automobile strong scores, including the top possible grade, in all of the crash tests that were conducted on it.

The Nissan Pathfinder model is equipped with lane departure warning, blind spot monitoring, and electronic stability control as standard features.

According to the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety, the Nissan Pathfinder was the seventh most stolen vehicle in the United States from 2007 to 2009.
