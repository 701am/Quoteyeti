## **About the Kia Rio**

The [Kia Rio](<https://www.kia.com/us/en/rio>) has been on the market since 2000, and its fourth-generation version is presently available. Because the subcompact car is recognized for its strong engine, luxury interior, and nimble handling, it's commonly described as fun to drive. There are two primary body styles of the Kia Rio in the United States: a hatchback and a sedan.

Insurance companies set the insurance cost for a Kia Rio according to many factors, including vehicle safety ratings, the likelihood of theft, the cost of replacement, and general damage susceptibility. Insurance premiums are dependent on the history and location of the motorist. If you want to obtain the lowest possible insurance rate, you should try to get quotations from many different insurance providers.

In addition, customers looking to purchase a Kia Rio should conduct thorough research to find out if they are eligible for insurance savings with each insurance provider.

## **How safe is the Kia Rio?**

The Insurance Institute for Highway Safety ([IIHS](<http://iihs.org>)) rated the Kia Rio an A+ for the moderate overlap front test, a C for the rear crash protection test, and an A for roof strength. For the side impact crash test, the IIHS gave the Kia Rio a marginal score due to its tiny overlap test result but gave it an acceptable score for the test overall. Overall, the Kia Rio received a 4-Star rating from the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration ([NHTSA](<http://nhtsa.gov>)). The 2017 Kia Rio received four stars for its frontal and rollover crash test results, both of which garnered a perfect score from the NHTSA. A separate vehicle inspection evaluated the side crash test for the Kia Rio and gave it five stars.

NHTSA estimated that in 2012, the Kia Rio was among the top 10 stolen automobiles in the country. According to the Federal Bureau of Investigation, there are about 2.7 thefts per 1,000 Kia Rio vehicles.
