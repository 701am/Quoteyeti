The average monthly premium for a [Nissan Juke](<https://www.nissanusa.com/vehicles/discontinued/juke.html>) insurance cost is $182, which translates to $2,184 in annual premiums. According to your driving record, the amount of miles you travel in a year, and other factors, your real cost may be more or lower than this.

## **About the Nissan Juke**

Nissan introduced the Juke in 2011. The subcompact SUV is distinguished by its dynamic handling and powerful turbocharged engine. In 2012, a stunt diver achieved a Guinness World Record using a Nissan Juke. The driver completed the Goodwood Hill Course in 2 minutes 55 seconds on two wheels.

Regrettably for Juke enthusiasts, Nissan has replaced this unique model with the 2018 Nissan Kicks. While the model remains popular internationally, sales in the United States are insufficient for Nissan to continue importing it for American drivers. If you currently possess a Juke, you need not be concerned about maintenance or insurance prices increasing, since most manufacturers continue to service defunct models such as the Juke for decades after they are no longer manufactured.

## **How safe is the Nissan Juke?**

The [Insurance Institute for Highway Safety (IIHS)](<https://www.iihs.org/ratings/vehicle/nissan/juke-4-door-hatchback/2017>) and the [National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA)](<https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle/2017/NISSAN/JUKE/SUV/AWD>) have assessed the 2017 Nissan Juke for safety ratings. The IIHS assigned a bad grade to the small overlap front crash test, the lowest attainable. The Nissan Juke got excellent ratings, the best attainable, in all other IIHS crash testing. The NHTSA awarded the automobile four stars out of five for its rollover crash test performance. Additionally, the government agency granted three stars for the frontal crash test.

Lane departure warning, blind spot monitoring, and electronic stability control are all standard on the Nissan Juke.
