Car battery maintenance is a common practice that most drivers need to perform regularly. Unfortunately, sometimes a dead cell may occur on the battery and prevent the car from running. This article will discuss the common causes of a dead cell and the steps you can take to fix the issue.

## What is a Dead Cell?

A dead cell is a part of the car battery that has completely lost its ability to maintain a charge. This occurs when the lead-acid chemical that composes the battery is unable to keep a charge, causing the battery to fail. The most common cause of a dead cell is corrosion, rust, or other damage to the battery, which can be caused by leaking acid, old age, or prolonged exposure to extreme temperatures.

## How to Diagnose a Dead Cell

Before attempting to fix a dead cell, you should first diagnose the battery to determine if a dead cell is the actual problem. The easiest way to do this is to use a multimeter and check the cell voltage. If the battery is unable to maintain a charge, then a dead cell is likely the cause. You can also inspect the battery cells for signs of corrosion or damage.

## How to Fix a Dead Cell

If the battery is unable to maintain a charge, then it is likely that a dead cell is the cause. To fix the issue, you will need to replace the entire battery. If the cell is only partially dead, then you may be able to restore it by charging the battery or adding an electrolyte solution. If the battery is completely dead, then it is best to replace it with a new one.

## Conclusion

Dead cells on a car battery can prevent the car from running and should be fixed as soon as possible. This article discussed the common causes of a dead cell and the steps you can take to fix the issue. If the battery is unable to maintain a charge, then it is likely that a dead cell is the cause and the battery should be replaced.
