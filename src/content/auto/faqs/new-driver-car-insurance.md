## How Much Is Car Insurance For A New Driver?

For a new driver who is 17 years old, annual automobile insurance might cost more than $5,500. When adding a 17-year-old novice driver to a parent's policy, auto insurance will set you back around $2,750 extra each year. However, the costs of insurance for new drivers are highly variable.

The cost of insurance varies depending on a driver's age. Not everyone gets their license for the first time at 16 or 17 years old.

A new driver maybe 25, 40, or even 60 years old. Insurance premiums vary depending on when someone obtains their driver's license.

They also take into account things like gender, state of residence, and coverage amounts. It is more costly to insure a new driver than it is to cover an experienced driver with excellent driving and insurance history.

## Observations about New Drivers Car Insurance

According to research, new drivers are more likely to have accidents and file claims than experienced drivers. As a result, insurance companies charge higher premiums to cover them. You can't fight facts, but you may take advantage of them in order to get the best deal possible. **The following observations may be drawn from a study of automobile insurance statistics:**

  * Adding a teenager to a parent's policy is less expensive. If you're a teen driver who lives at home or in a dorm, ask your parents to add you to their policy. It will be about half as pricey as having your own insurance coverage.
  * Luxury vehicles are costly to repair, and sports vehicles are frequently driven at high speeds. Driving a less expensive, practical automobile with adequate safety and anti-theft features can help you save money on your insurance premiums.
  * Students with exceptional marks are better drivers. Insurance providers provide price reductions to students with a B average or higher.
  * Driving lessons help drivers improve their skills. Many insurance carriers provide discounts for driver's education or internet defensive driving courses.
  * Teachers, physicians, policemen, and other well-educated specialists file fewer claims. Some insurance companies provide reductions based on employment.
