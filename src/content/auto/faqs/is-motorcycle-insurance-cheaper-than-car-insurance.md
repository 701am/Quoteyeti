Motorcycle insurance is, on average, less expensive than auto insurance. However, there are exceptions. Depending on your location and the value of your motorcycle, you may find that you pay more for insurance than you would for a car.

Motorcycles are an excellent mode of transportation, but they are also more dangerous to ride than automobiles. Having the appropriate insurance coverage in place when you need it could be the difference between life and death.

## What is the cost of motorcycle insurance?

Motorcycles, on average, cost less than half the monthly cost of vehicle insurance.

## Why are motorcycles more affordable to insure than automobiles?

Motorcycles are significantly less expensive to insure than automobiles for a variety of reasons.

**They are often less expensive than automobiles to purchase.**

They are often less expensive to fix and replace than other types of vehicles, resulting in cheaper insurance costs.

**They are less prone to cause property damage in an accident.**

Insurance companies examine factors such as safety ratings when setting premiums. Additionally, while automobiles are generally considered to be safer to drive than motorbikes, even vehicles with great safety ratings are frequently more expensive to insure.

## Is it possible for motorcycle insurance to be more expensive than auto insurance?

A motorcycle can cost more than a vehicle, particularly if it is a high-end model.

Your insurance rate is significantly affected by the type of motorcycle you ride. If you own a costly, rare, or vintage model, replacement components will be more difficult to come by and will be more expensive. The state in which you reside might also affect the cost of motorbike insurance.

## Don't forget about the cost of medical expenses

While motorcycles are less expensive to insure than cars, they are not significantly safer to drive. Motorcycle riders are up to five times more likely to be harmed than other drivers, according to National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) statistics.

**Having supplemental medical coverage is generally a good idea if you ride a motorcycle.**

If you live in a no-fault state, you may not be required to obtain medical payment or PIP coverage. This means that if you are involved in an accident, you may be held financially responsible for medical expenses.
